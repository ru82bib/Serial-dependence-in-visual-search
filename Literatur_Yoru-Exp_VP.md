# Literature: Visual Priming and Serial Dependence

I think the following paper could be a good point of orientation because
they examined visual priming (PoP) and serial dependence within the same
experiment.

## Visual Priming and Serial Dependence Are Mediated by Separate Mechanisms

**Authors:** Filippo Galluzzi, Alessandro Benedetto, Guido Marco
Cicchini, and David C. Burr (2022)\
**Journal:** *Journal of Vision*, 22(10), 1--12.\
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1167/jov.22.10.1

## Experimental tasks

Participants had two tasks:

1.  Identify the odd-colored patch (visual priming task).
2.  Reproduce the orientation of that patch by moving their finger
    across a trackpad (serial dependence task).

*The diagram on page 1 illustrates the sequence of stimulus displays and
the trackpad response.*

## Independent variables

Two IVs:

-   **Target-Color:** Did the target color repeat or change?
-   **Target-Position:** Did the target appear in the same position or a
    different position?

## Main findings

1.  Repeating color and position improved reaction times.
2.  Orientation judgments were attracted toward the previous stimulus.
3.  Priming did not significantly influence serial dependence.
4.  Priming and serial dependence were not correlated.

## The central conclusion

Visual priming and serial dependence are two distinct processes through
which perceptual history influences current visual processing.

## Number of trials

-   Each participant: approximately 15 blocks × 100 trials = 1,500
    trials in total.
-   Each block took approximately 4 minutes.

> Although our IVs are target-color and distractor-color, we still have
> the same number of IVs. However, our study should only be 30 min so
> probably we have to do less trials. In their study they used so many
> trials also to look at effects of consecutive repetition.

## Main difference to our study

They did not look at the effect of the distractor-repetition. So, we
could try replicating maybe some findings of their work and at the same
time extend the research by looking also at the distractor-inhibition
mechanism.

## Also ...

-   We must not repeat the target-position of consecutive trials,
    otherwise it could confound our results due to spatial priming
    (Maljkovic & Nakayama (1996): *Priming of pop-out: II. The role of
    position*, DOI: 10.3758/bf03206826).
